# Xtorra Renewables — Experience Direction

## Three visual approaches considered

### Sunfield Ledger
**Very Brief Intro:** An editorial, field-notebook approach that presents clean energy with measured confidence, mixing sun-washed space with precise technical detail. It makes Xtorra feel like a serious delivery partner rather than a generic green brand.

**Probability:** 0.07

### Solar Cartography
**Very Brief Intro:** A composed, topographic visual language inspired by solar azimuth charts, land contours, and the supplied radial mark. It feels optimistic and engineered at once, turning energy flow into a recognizable visual system.

**Probability:** 0.03

### Vital Grid
**Very Brief Intro:** A dark operational interface that frames renewables as a living energy network, with sharp information panels and electric accents. It is bold and technical, leaning toward a control-room sensibility.

**Probability:** 0.09

## Chosen direction: Solar Cartography

### Design Movement
**Solar Cartography** combines contemporary editorial environmental design with the analytical calm of topographic mapping. The supplied sunburst mark becomes a recurring visual language for pathways, rays, and measurement arcs—used with restraint, not decoration.

### Core Principles
1. **Engineered optimism:** Bright, grounded materials and exact typographic rhythm make clean energy feel credible and forward-looking.
2. **Directional composition:** Content flows left-to-right and along subtle arcs, avoiding default centered stacks and echoing a solar trajectory.
3. **Purposeful restraint:** Dense information is separated by generous whitespace, clear labels, and limited, high-value emphasis.
4. **Tactile clarity:** Fine grain, hairline rules, soft shadows, and paper-like surfaces offer depth without visual noise.

### Color Philosophy
The white of the original logo is treated as a daylight canvas. Deep **Xtorra Navy** establishes trust and engineering rigor; a signature **Solar Lime** carries active, renewable energy; a warm **Sun Amber** appears selectively to signal momentum; and pale mineral blue gives sections a calm, breathable field. Dark blue is for authority, not moodiness; lime is for action, not garnish.

### Layout Paradigm
The site is a **narrative field guide** rather than a conventional centered brochure. A slim editorial rail appears on large screens, sections alternate between wide image fields and annotated content bands, and the service story moves along a horizontal energy path. Cards use offset placements and overlapping datums rather than a rigid uniform grid.

### Signature Elements
1. **Azimuth arcs:** Thin radial strokes based on the logo’s sunburst suggest energy moving outward.
2. **Field labels:** Small uppercase labels and coordinate-like markers establish a technical, project-ready voice.
3. **Gradient energy line:** A restrained navy-to-lime line joins key information blocks and anchors active states.

### Interaction Philosophy
Every interaction should make orientation easier: navigation highlights the current field of view, service cards reveal practical next steps, and an enquiry panel focuses the user on a short decision path. Hover effects behave like instruments responding to touch—direct, light, and informative.

### Animation
At page entry, content comes into view with an upward 16px drift and 0.45s opacity transition, staggered by 60ms. The azimuth arcs may rotate a few degrees on hover and the energy line can draw across a section once, but motion never loops continuously. Buttons press down by 3% and release sharply. All decorative motion is disabled for reduced-motion preferences.

### Typography System
**DM Serif Display** gives sectional headlines an editorial, field-report authority, while **Manrope** keeps body copy and controls precise and highly readable. Headlines use a wide, near-tight scale; labels are uppercase Manrope with generous tracking; paragraphs remain short and unforced. No generic system-like display typography.

### Brand Essence
**Xtorra Renewables helps organisations move from energy ambition to dependable clean-power action through clear, practical renewable solutions.**

**Personality:** considered, capable, forward-looking.

### Brand Voice
The voice is concise, specific, and assured. Headlines state a productive outcome; CTAs invite a practical conversation; microcopy reduces friction and avoids overpromising.

> “Make clean power part of your operating advantage.”

> “Tell us where energy is holding you back.”

### Wordmark & Logo
The supplied Xtorra Renewables logo is the primary wordmark and visual authority. A simplified sun-ray symbol, derived from its radial geometry without text, will support the favicon and small interface moments. The full wordmark remains unobstructed on white or pale mineral surfaces.

### Signature Brand Color
**Solar Lime — #58A90E.** A bright, vegetation-forward green used selectively for active states, directional marks, and primary actions.

## Style Decisions

- The hero introduces the Solar Cartography system with a visible field route, radial azimuth arc, coordinate stamp, and active route point derived from the Xtorra sunburst geometry.
- Renewable imagery is treated as **field evidence** through consistent navy document tags, coordinate paths, route lines, and radial measurement overlays.
- Solar Lime remains reserved for active actions, route points, key numerals, and directional measurement signals rather than general decoration.
- Every major content band carries at least one orientation device—field-station stamp, datum label, route line, azimuth arc, or measured output tag—so the experience reads as one mapped energy journey.
- Calculator and form-heavy areas are treated as annotated field instruments rather than generic software panels, with visible measurement hierarchy and operational labels.
- Renewable imagery is consistently documented as field evidence through navy tags, route paths, measurement marks, and concise project-record captions.
- Long calculator areas are treated as **field instruments**: each has one dominant measured-output moment, smaller datum readings, and a deliberately limited Solar Lime signal hierarchy.
- Field-route lines now cross major transitions, especially from the savings field into the system-design station and onward to site evidence, so navigation reads as one continuous energy map.
- Later imagery carries compact coordinate or azimuth stamps as well as route labels, keeping the field-evidence language consistent from hero through sales enquiry.
- The system-design workbench carries a visible radial route legend from coordinates through load, loss, and equipment screening, reinforced by sunburst-derived arcs and ray marks.
- The sales enquiry is the final **field station** rather than a generic contact form: it includes a route-terminus tag, coordinate-record header, and a radial Xtorra geometry cue that closes the mapped journey.
- Dedicated calculator pages use a continuous route language across editable datum panels, measured output cards, yield evidence, BoQ records, methodology notes, and route terminus rather than presenting a stack of generic dashboards.
- Utility-scale workbenches use tactile planning surfaces—contour-like evidence lines, radial sunburst marks, datum labels, and sparse route spines—to make site-scale planning feel observed and physical, not abstract or administrative.
- The system-design workbench is an explicit **field instrument**: its measured load, energy profile, and route legend receive a stronger visual hierarchy than editable controls, with solar lime reserved for live readings and directional progress.
- A simplified radial sunburst functions as an active route-point and measurement symbol across the workbench and project evidence, while compact datum coordinates make every case-study image read as an observed field record.
- The header functions as the opening edge of the field guide, carrying a compact origin coordinate and route line alongside the authoritative Xtorra wordmark.
- Long workbench sequences retain a continuous perimeter route spine with sparse datum labels and active route points, ensuring the calculator reads as a mapped journey rather than a stack of dashboard cards.
- Measured outputs remain visually primary in instrument areas; editable controls are secondary datum inputs and Solar Lime is reserved for active energy, route points, and decision-critical readings.
- The header carries the same field-guide datum as the workbench—radial origin marker, route segment, and concise guide label—so Xtorra authority begins before the first interaction.
- The long workbench is held together by a legible perimeter route spine, a header-scale azimuth trace, and high-contrast measured-output bands; these cues prevent editable panels from reading as generic dashboard cards.
- Case evidence transitions use route-aligned rails and radial points to carry visitors from measured inputs toward documented energy pathways, while Solar Lime stays restricted to live readings and directional decisions.

- The first fold must function as an **opening map plate**: a numbered origin, visible route segment, azimuth geometry, field label, and evidence-image station connect the Xtorra wordmark, headline, and first decision action.
- The system-design route opens with a high-contrast live-reading band for demand, solar resource, and reserve, so measured results establish the hierarchy before the user reaches editable datum inputs.
- Operational CTAs use field-guide language—opening a station, inspecting evidence, or following a route—rather than generic browsing or enquiry phrases.
- Long workbench sequences must never present more than one screenful of equal-weight white panels without a dominant measured-output station or cartographic route marker resetting the hierarchy.
- The sunburst-derived azimuth mark is the primary proprietary motif and appears at major transitions as a route point, measurement cue, or field-record stamp rather than background decoration.
- Operational UI copy uses field-guide verbs and evidence language, avoiding generic SaaS phrasing unless it is paired with mapped-energy context.
